Examples of recent modifications

A selection of modifications ordered at PPR Ltd. If the modification you require isn't listed here, get in touch — these are only examples of what we can perform.

Acetylation & amidation →

N-terminal acetyl capping and C-terminal amidation — two of the most common ways to block reactive peptide termini and improve stability.

Biotinylated peptides →

Biotin conjugation at the N-terminus or on a Lys/Cys side chain, for streptavidin-based capture, detection and purification.

Cyclisation →

Head-to-tail, side-chain and disulfide cyclisation strategies for constrained, conformationally locked peptide designs.

D-amino acids →

Non-natural D-stereochemistry incorporated in place of standard L-amino acids to resist proteolytic degradation.

Fluorescent labelled peptides →

FAM, TAMRA, Cy dyes and Alexa Fluor conjugates for imaging, FRET assays and flow cytometry applications.

Phosphorylated serine, threonine and tyrosine →

Site-specific phosphorylation for kinase substrate studies, signalling research and phospho-specific antibody generation.

Peptide aldehydes →

C-terminal aldehyde functionality, commonly used for oxime ligation, reductive amination and protease inhibitor design.

N-methyl amino acids →

Backbone N-methylation to reduce hydrogen bonding, improve membrane permeability and resist enzymatic cleavage.

Thiols →

Free or protected cysteine thiol groups, used as chemoselective handles for conjugation and native chemical ligation.

Unnatural amino acids →

Non-standard residues — including Aib, Cha, Nle, Nal and isotopically labelled variants — incorporated where your design calls for them.

Urea bond peptides →

Urea linkages incorporated as a peptide-bond isostere, improving proteolytic stability at specific positions in the backbone.

Our chemists are available to discuss strategy before you commit to a design — particularly for complex or combined modifications.
